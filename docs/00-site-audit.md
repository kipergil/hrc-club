# Site Audit — hertsttl.org.uk

**Audited:** 26 August 2026
**Method:** full crawl from `http://hertsttl.org.uk/Home.htm`, 90 URLs retrieved, ~40 distinct page templates, plus stylesheet and JavaScript inspection.
**Season in progress at time of audit:** 2026‑27 (pre‑season; no results entered yet, so several pages show "too early" states).

---

## 1. What the site is

The public website of the Hertford & District Table Tennis League — founded 1936, celebrating its 90th year, 10 clubs, 26 teams, 3 divisions, affiliated to Table Tennis England and the Hertfordshire County TTA. It is the league's operational system, not just a brochure: fixtures, results entry, tables, averages, handicaps, cup draws, tournament entries and committee documents all live here.

The site is maintained by a volunteer webmaster and a match secretary.

## 2. Current technology

| Layer | What is there |

| Server | Microsoft IIS 10.0, ASP.NET, Plesk for Windows |
| Application | Classic ASP (`.asp`) reading a database, plus hand‑generated `.htm` files |
| Static generation | Fixture calendars are generated by a Windows Scripting Host script (per a comment in `TTLStyles.css`) |
| Markup | Microsoft FrontPage 6.0 / Word 9 export; `xmlns:o="urn:schemas-microsoft-com:office:office"` namespaces throughout |
| Encoding | `WINDOWS-1252` |

| Transport | HTTP only — HTTPS presents a certificate that does not match the hostname |

## 4. Findings

Ordered by severity. Each is referenced by ID in the PRD.

### Critical

**A1 — No working HTTPS.** `https://hertsttl.org.uk` fails certificate validation (`no alternative certificate subject name matches target host name`). Every visitor is on plain HTTP; modern browsers label the site "Not secure", and the login and entry forms transmit email addresses in clear text. Google demotes HTTP sites in search.

**A2 — Authentication is an email address with no password.** Both `LogMeIn.asp` (players) and `SignIn.asp` (admin) ask only for an email address, then set a cookie. Anyone who knows or guesses a committee member's email address — several are printed on the site — can enter admin mode. This also means the "LogIn to see" gate on player phone numbers and email addresses offers no real protection: the contact details of every captain and player are effectively public.

**A3 — Personal data handling.** Names, emails and phone numbers of players (including juniors — there is a Junior Singles event) are held behind that non‑gate. There is no privacy notice, no stated lawful basis, no retention policy and no erasure route. This is a UK GDPR exposure for the committee as data controller.

**A4 — Forms submit by GET.** The login, feedback and tournament‑entry forms all use `Method="GET"`, putting email addresses into URLs, browser history, referrer headers and server access logs.

### High

**B1 — The site is not usable on a phone.** Not one of the 90 pages carries a `<meta name="viewport">` tag. Every page renders as a desktop layout scaled down; a fixture grid 16 columns wide is unreadable on a handset. Given that a large share of players will check fixtures from a phone in a sports hall, this is the single biggest usability gap.

**B2 — Navigation is hover‑only.** `TTmenu.js` opens submenus on `onmouseover` and closes them on `onmouseout`. On a touch device the submenus cannot be opened at all. For an older audience on a laptop, the menu snaps shut if the pointer drifts off the strip — a common problem for anyone with a tremor or reduced fine motor control. There is no keyboard path to any submenu item.

**B3 — Type and colour choices work against older eyes.** Body copy is set in Comic Sans at fixed point sizes; headings run to 36–40pt while table data sits at 8–10pt. Crimson and salmon table headers on beige and ivory backgrounds fall well below WCAG contrast minimums. Point‑based sizing means browser text zoom behaves unpredictably.

**B4 — Meaning carried by formatting alone.** Away fixtures are indicated by *italics*, cup weeks by a beige cell, holidays by a silver cell, and "played up/down" players by italics. None of this is announced to a screen reader, and italic‑vs‑roman at 8pt is not a distinction most 70‑year‑olds can make reliably.

**B5 — Broken links on the primary navigation.** `HCapApp1.asp` (Handicap Competition) and `ClosedForm2019-20.htm` (Closed Form) both 404 from the home page menu.

**B6 — Duplicate URLs for the same club.** `Clubz.asp?Club=Water Lane` and `Clubz.asp?Club=WaterLane` both return 200 with different content — the space‑less variant is ~3 KB smaller and missing data. Same pattern for Grundy Park, St. Andrews, Stanstead Abbotts and Furneux Pelham. Search engines see duplicate content; users can land on the degraded copy.

### Medium

**C1 — Stale hardcoded logic.** `Home.htm` contains inline JavaScript comparing today's date to `13 December 2025`–`26 December 2025` to decide whether to show Christmas decorations. In the 2026‑27 season page this is a year out of date, and an animated Christmas GIF plus a "lightstring" GIF ship on every load in August.

**C2 — Null‑date and null‑text bugs surfacing to visitors.** `ClosedInvite.asp` reads "All applications to be in by Wednesday, December 27, 1899" (an unset date rendering as the epoch) and "to be held at restrictions, there are no current plans for Closed Championships…" (a venue field holding a fragment of a sentence). `ClosedList.asp` repeats both.

**C3 — Encoding damage.** WINDOWS‑1252 content served with mismatched escapes produces visible mojibake: "Jo� Swain" in the Roll of Honour, "w/c 14th Sept�corrected Handbooks" in the newsletter.

**C4 — Internal working notes leaking into the page.** `Tables.asp` emits a long generated paragraph — "In the Premier Division Kidston tied with Water Lane A on 0 points but lost on these criteria…" repeated for every pair of teams — into the markup as tooltip content. At the start of a season with all teams on zero it produces 24 nonsense sentences.

**C5 — Content rot.** Committee minutes are from 2017. The handicap and closed‑competition forms are the 2019‑20 editions. The blank league scorecard was authored in 2009. Newsletter №99 on the current‑season page is dated 7 Sep 2023.

**C6 — Feedback channel closed by spam.** The league explicitly wants to hear why player numbers are falling and why AGM attendance has dropped — and the only form for saying so has been switched off. There is no spam mitigation in place to switch it back on.

**C7 — Archives are hand‑copied files, not queries.** `Tables2024.htm`, `Tables2025.htm`, `averages2024.htm`, `Averages2025.htm`, `Handicaps2025.htm`, `Matches2025.htm` are separate documents. Every season adds more files to maintain by hand and more chances for the "click here for last season" links to point at the wrong year.

**C8 — Page weight and markup bloat.** Word‑exported pages carry thousands of `mso-*` declarations. The Hall of Fame is 122 KB of markup for a table; the averages archive is 79 KB. On a slow mobile connection these are slow, and none of it is cached aggressively.

### Low

**D1 — No `robots.txt`, no XML sitemap, no Open Graph or structured data.** Sharing a fixture link to WhatsApp produces a bare URL with no title or image.
**D2 — `index.htm` returns 404.** The root works via `default.htm`, but any link or bookmark to `/index.htm` fails.
**D3 — No search.** With ~40 page types and decades of honours, finding "when did Hoddesdon last win the Creasey Cup" means scrolling a 122 KB grid.
**D4 — Print instructions as UI.** Several pages tell the user "Press Ctrl & P to print (Landscape is best)" instead of providing a print button and a print stylesheet — an accurate signal that printing matters to this audience, and that it is unsupported.
**D5 — Advertising and disclaimer text is mixed into the page body** rather than a defined sponsor slot, so it competes with content for attention.

## 5. What is working well and should be preserved

An audit that only lists faults will mislead the rebuild. These things are good and are load‑bearing for the existing audience:

- **The information architecture is broadly right.** Tables, fixtures, clubs, averages, handicaps, cups, documents — the league's real domains are all represented and the labels are the ones players use.
- **"Back to HomePage" on every single page.** It is repetitive and it works. Older users rely on a guaranteed way back. Keep an equivalent.
- **Captains enter their own results.** The distributed data‑entry model already exists and is understood; the rebuild should make it easier, not replace it with a central bottleneck.
- **Explanatory prose next to the data.** Cup rules are stated on the cup page. The tie‑break rule is quoted from Handbook rule 20 with a worked example. This is genuinely helpful and rare.
- **Everything is on one domain with short, guessable, stable URLs.** Bookmarks made years ago still work.
- **Print‑ready scorecards are available in several formats.** The formats are dated, but the need is real.
- **The site is current.** Last updated three days before this audit. Whoever maintains it is diligent.

## 6. Data the site already holds

Recoverable from the existing site and database, and needed by any replacement:

- 10 clubs with venues, addresses, postcodes, map links
- 26 teams across 3 divisions, with home night and captain
- Full 2026‑27 fixture list for all divisions (week‑commencing scheduling)
- Player registrations by team, with "played up/down" flags
- Match results at scorecard level (individual rubbers, sets)
- League tables with a documented tie‑break rule
- Player averages (played / won / lost / percentage), with a 50% participation threshold for eligibility
- Handicap ratings per player per season
- Four cup competitions with distinct formats (Creasey Cup, Clifford Troll Trophy, MSD Trophy, Hertford Builders Trophy)
- Closed Championship with 13 events and online entry
- **Honours back to 1970** — the single most valuable and least replaceable asset on the site
- Roll of Honour per season, including individual awards (Pine Trophy, Wadesmill Trophy, Special Award)
- Newsletters, notices, committee minutes, constitution, handbook, registration forms
- Committee roster with roles
